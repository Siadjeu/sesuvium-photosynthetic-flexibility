Daytime tissue differences in supported family transcripts and metabolite pools

Of 99 supported S. sesuvioides loci across 18 families, 83 had assessable daytime leaf-versus-cotyledon differential-expression results. Forty-two met the primary criteria (within-contrast adjusted p < 0.05 and absolute shrunken log2 fold change >= 1), with 21 more highly expressed in leaves and 21 more highly expressed in cotyledons. Sixteen unassessable loci were retained in the evidence tables and omitted from the revised plots.

The NADP-ME family showed heterogeneous expression across tissues. Two genes, g28809 and g8625, were more highly expressed in leaves (shrunken log2 fold changes 2.615 and 2.555), whereas g1355, g6264 and g6698 were less highly expressed in leaves (-1.260, -1.243 and -1.964). All five met the primary DE criteria; g31795 was unassessable. Among five assessable NAD-ME genes, g4479 met the primary criteria and was higher in leaves (log2 fold change 1.001; adjusted p = 0.0161). Five MDH genes met the primary criteria, comprising one higher and four lower in leaves.

In the independent metabolomics cohort, mean leaf/cotyledon relative-abundance ratios were 0.192 for pyruvate, 0.175 for fumarate and 0.329 for 2-oxoglutarate. Each difference met the six-test BH threshold (adjusted p = 0.0238). Succinate was lower on average in leaves (ratio 0.339), but its adjusted p-value was 0.0536. Malate and citrate had higher leaf means (ratios 83.27 and 8.16), with adjusted p-values of 0.0714 and 0.2143, respectively. The malate ratio requires caution because cotyledon values were near a suspected preprocessing floor and its comparison did not pass the adjusted 0.05 threshold.

No tissue-adjusted metabolite correlation passed BH-adjusted p < 0.05 across 15 pairs. The strongest adjusted rank association was malate–citrate (r = 0.911; permutation p = 0.00509; BH-adjusted p = 0.0764). Succinate–2-oxoglutarate had r = 0.855 (p = 0.01296; adjusted p = 0.0972). These associations remain exploratory.

The reaction-based comparison yielded 39 locus–metabolite records: 14 same-sign, 21 opposite-sign and four unassessable. These are repeated descriptive links, not independent biological tests. Seven records had both an upstream primary-DE gene call and a metabolite comparison passing its separate adjusted threshold; this was not a joint association test. The results document tissue-dependent transcript abundance and differences in independently measured metabolite pools. They do not establish gene–metabolite correlations, causal regulation, metabolic flux or CAM-specific function.
