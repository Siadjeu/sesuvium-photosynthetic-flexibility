Daytime transcript–metabolite comparison in Sesuvium sesuvioides

Previously generated RNA-seq differential-expression results were integrated descriptively with metabolite relative-abundance measurements from independent plants. Metabolomics comprised three independent cotyledon samples (C1–C3) and six independent leaf samples (SS1–SS6), all collected during daytime. SP_tetra samples were excluded. RNA-seq comprised three biological replicates per group; the matched-condition comparison used leaves_day as numerator and cotyledons_day as denominator. The transcriptome also included cotyledons_night, used only in the earlier three-group expression heatmaps. No cross-assay sample pairing was assumed.

The supported-family ledger contained 99 SS loci across 18 families. Existing apeglm-shrunken log2 fold changes and within-contrast adjusted p-values were reused without refitting differential-expression models. An assessable result required finite values for both quantities. Primary DE required adjusted p < 0.05 and absolute shrunken log2 fold change >= 1, as implemented in the original supported-family integration script. Plotting retained the 83 assessable genes with finite effects, while all 99 loci remained in the evidence tables. Filled dots denote primary DE; open dots denote assessable results failing at least one primary criterion. Thus an open dot may have adjusted p < 0.05. Positive effects indicate higher leaf expression. Labels were placed beyond dots in the direction of the effect.

The supplied metabolite matrix contained 15 samples and 82 measurement columns. The SS analysis excluded 31 columns representing amino acids/related compounds or ambiguous duplicated identities, retaining 51 columns in the audit output. Both Phenylalanine columns were excluded. Salicin and beta-Galactopyranosyl-1,3-arabinose, which were identical across all supplied samples, were excluded rather than assigning an identity arbitrarily. Six non-amino-acid organic acids were selected before significance testing for the focused display: malate, pyruvate, citrate, fumarate, succinate and 2-oxoglutarate. Values were treated as relative abundance. No additional normalization, imputation or pseudocount was applied. Repeated low values were documented but not recoded because their origin was unconfirmed.

For each selected metabolite, tissue differences were evaluated using the absolute difference between arithmetic means and all 84 allocations of nine samples to groups of three and six. The two-sided exact permutation p-value was the fraction of allocations with an absolute mean difference at least as large as observed. This test assumes exchangeability under the null and does not correct unknown batch effects. Benjamini–Hochberg (BH) adjustment was performed across the six metabolite comparisons. Replicate plots show individual plants and mean +/- sample SD. Comparative effect plots use log2(mean leaf abundance / mean cotyledon abundance), a different estimand from shrunken RNA-seq log2 fold change.

Exploratory metabolite–metabolite associations were calculated as pooled Spearman correlations across the nine SS metabolomics samples. For tissue adjustment, values were ranked globally, within-tissue mean ranks were subtracted, and Pearson correlations of the residual ranks were calculated. All 3! x 6! = 4,320 within-tissue permutations of one variable were evaluated for each pair; two-sided p-values used absolute correlations. BH adjustment was applied separately across the 15 distinct metabolite pairs. These analyses were performed in the metabolite release and reused for reporting.

Family-level reaction associations were compiled from IUBMB enzyme entries for MDH (representative NAD-dependent reaction, EC 1.1.1.37), NAD-ME (1.1.1.39), NADP-ME (1.1.1.40), PPDK (2.7.9.1), ASPAT (2.6.1.1) and AlaAT (2.6.1.2). Nine family–metabolite links generated 39 locus–metabolite records. Reaction roles refer to the reaction as written and do not establish in-vivo direction, cofactor specificity of every MDH locus, compartment or enzyme activity. Amino-acid names in reaction equations are contextual; amino-acid measurements were excluded. Families without a curated direct link in this selected panel were labeled metabolic context only. Same/opposite effect signs were recorded descriptively, with no joint significance test or gene–metabolite correlation calculation.

The computational consolidation reuses the two completed releases without executing sequence analyses, RNA-seq fitting or statistical tests. Input and output integrity is recorded through SHA256 manifests; software versions are preserved in each source QC.json. Metabolomics acquisition, compound identification confidence, normalization and detection-limit handling must be supplied from laboratory records before final manuscript submission.
